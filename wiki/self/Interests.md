# Interests

SENAIDA's interests are unusually integrated with their work — most intellectual fascinations become research projects, artworks, or companies. The line between "curious about" and "actively making" is thin. This page covers the intellectual and aesthetic terrain that shapes their practice.

## Electronic music and global club culture

The deepest and most central interest. Not just as a practitioner — SENAIDA approaches club culture as a theorist and archivist, drawing on their role as a "cyberfeminist scholar and archivist." Their listening spans contemporary techno, acid, avant-garde electronic music, and a wide range of global dance music traditions. The Operator Radio guest mix (October 2025) was framed around "the post-club narrative" and "socio-economic implications for the rise and commercialization of techno" — this is not a casual DJ's framing.

## Feminist theory and cyberfeminism

Donna Haraway's cyborg theory and Legacy Russell's Glitch Feminism are explicit intellectual anchors. SENAIDA's Medium essay on sonic cyberfeminism identifies eight future domains for change in music technology, from intersectional instrument design to ethics in algorithmic composition. This is academic engagement, not just political alignment. The framework animates nearly everything else: the critique of AI, the design of KUNST KAPUTT's business model, the founding of queer BIPOC spaces.

## Neurotechnology and biofeedback

Begun as an undergraduate research project (MiSynth, 2020) and developed into the Cyborg Synchrony paper (2025), this is a serious ongoing intellectual interest. The core question: how does the body — brainwaves, heartbeat, physiology — interface with sound production and collective experience? Their live performances using Muse biosensor headbands (heartbeat as kick drum) suggest the interest is both theoretical and directly embodied in practice.

## Generative AI and music

Less about AI as a tool, more about AI as a site of political and aesthetic contestation. KUNST KAPUTT is the clearest statement: a participatory generative album that uses blockchain revenue-sharing and AI remixing to critique streaming exploitation. SENAIDA was on the "Generative AI & Music" panel at New Visions for Music & Sound (Berlin, 2024). The interest is genuinely sophisticated — they're engaging with music game theory, human-AI collaboration aesthetics, and the ethics of algorithmic composition simultaneously.

## Shamanism and ritual

Referenced explicitly in the art bio ("shamanism") and in how SENAIDA describes their DJ practice ("shaman of the dancefloor," "clubbing as sacred ritual"). The interest in liminal states, collective transformation, and threshold experiences runs through the installation work (*Like Water, Like Clouds*, *And So, We Let the Tears of Our Grief Wash Over Our Bodies*) and the club practice. Worth exploring further — how does a cyberfeminist relationship to shamanism actually work? Is it metaphorical or ontological?

## Chinese and Hakka cultural heritage

The project *<Hakka> means Guest People* and the recurring self-identification as "Hakka Chinese-Canadian" signal a sustained engagement with heritage and diaspora. The Hakka etymology ("guest people") resonates with the experience of constant geographic movement and cultural in-between-ness. The **Hakka Futurism** research project (active, in Notion) develops this into a full intellectual and artistic framework — journal entries, a manifesto mindmap, a 6-week language exploration, and research leads on Hakka diaspora history. The closest structural analogue is Alan Poma's Andean Futurism — both are artists building futurist manifestos from non-Western, historically marginalized cultural traditions. See [[Concepts/Hakka-Futurism]].

## Posthumanism and transhumanism

The art bio names posthumanism explicitly; the NFT collection *YESTERDAY, I BECAME A CYBORG* names transhumanism directly. The Cyborg Synchrony paper is implicitly posthumanist — designing systems where human physiology and AI music generation are constitutively entangled. The interest in the human-machine interface runs from the earliest work (MiSynth, 2020) through the most recent research.

## Sustainability

Named in the art bio and the about page as a core concern. Shows up most explicitly in *The Last Rice Plant Harvested By Human Hands* (agriculture and labor) and the general framing of art and technology as tools for "transcendent collective experiences." The specific connection between sustainability and sonic practice is worth developing — this feels like an underspecified interest that might deepen in future work.

## Buddhism

An emerging interest noted explicitly. Given the existing interests in ritual, liminal states, shamanism, and collective transcendence, Buddhism is a natural extension — but it's also potentially distinct from those. How Buddhist practice (meditation, philosophy, ethics) interfaces with the existing cyberfeminist framework and technological orientation is worth tracking as it develops. The morning meditation practice may be one expression of this.

## Astrology

Named as a personal interest; astrological profile is Cancer Sun / Libra Moon / Scorpio Rising (July 16, 2002). Whether this is a casual or serious engagement is not yet clear — but it appears in the list of focus areas alongside language learning and Buddhism, suggesting genuine investment.

## Language learning

SENAIDA is active in five languages at very different levels:
- **Chinese (Mandarin):** Advanced vocabulary level — the most developed non-English language, consistent with Hakka heritage and the recurring China touring circuit
- **German:** A2 level with significant grammar loss — lived in Berlin but the language hasn't become fluent; actively wants to restore what's been forgotten
- **Hindi:** Beginner — consistent with the India touring life and the four-month Chennai stay with Ricky's family
- **Thai:** Beginner — consistent with future plans (Wonderfruit Pattaya, possible Thailand relocation)
- **Greek:** Beginner — Athens was a recent stop (May 2026); Greece is a stated ideal location

The breadth of languages being actively learned maps directly onto the travel circuit and the five-year vision. This is not casual — five simultaneous languages suggests a genuine cognitive interest in language alongside the practical motivation.

## Music discovery and curation

Beyond performing, SENAIDA wants to maintain active music discovery through SoundCloud playlist curation — a signal that the listening practice is considered distinct from and important to the performing practice. Good curators tend to listen obsessively to the fringes; playlist-making is a way of staying close to the culture.

## Reading

Primary interest in non-fiction. Currently finishing *Machine Decision is Not Final*. Has a large desktop Reading List folder of books and articles to work through. Reading as a sustained practice connects to the research background and the interest in frameworks that make sense of the world — cyberfeminism, biofeedback, neurotechnology, sound healing.

## Investing

An emerging practical interest: ETFs and bonds specifically. This connects directly to the financial freedom by 30 goal — SENAIDA is not just thinking about income, but about building wealth through investing. Beginning-to-intermediate level of engagement.

## Media theory and philosophy of technology

An increasingly serious intellectual interest, fed by this batch of reading. Key figures and frameworks: Vilém Flusser's **technical images** (the shift from denotative text-based communication to connotative image-based communication, and what this means for truth, authority, and democracy — see [[Concepts/Technical-Images]]); Yuk Hui's **cosmotechnics** (the idea that different cultures have different technological imaginations shaped by their cosmological traditions); Benjamin Bratton's **planetary computation** (AI as civilizational-scale infrastructure, not just a product). These frameworks connect the AI interests to broader questions about knowledge, governance, and what different cultures bring to technology.

## Political philosophy

An intellectual current running through several recent readings: Marxist analysis of automation and alienation (Tony Burns's paper on recognition theory, automation, and republican citizenship); critique of consumer individualism (Curtis's *Century of the Self*); the political economy of music infrastructure (Water & Music on PE consolidation). These are not just academic interests but connect to her activist commitments and her critique of the music industry's extractive models.

## AI critique and counter-AI methodology

Distinct from the generative AI interest above — this is the critical and analytical wing. Eryk Salvaggio's cluster of work (Cybernetic Forests newsletter) is the primary voice: AI as ideology, the inner life problem, glitch as methodology, alternative architectures. See [[Concepts/AI-Critique]] for the full framework. Caroline Busta's technical images essay and Jan Kulveit's three-layer LLM psychology model are adjacent.

## Sound architecture & Deep Listening

The intersection of acoustic theory, spatial design, and embodied listening. Bernhard Leitner's 1971 foundational work; Xenakis's stochastic composition and the Philips Pavilion; Yidi Wang's artificial synaesthesia concept. SENAIDA has an active Notion research project on this, including workshop engagement and research into ceramic speakers and biopsychological acoustic experiments. Central question: how does music become an embodied imaginative experience? See [[Concepts/Sonic-Architecture]].

**Pauline Oliveros's Deep Listening** (from the Inspiration Board) is a foundational reference in this cluster. Oliveros developed Deep Listening as both a compositional practice and a somatic discipline — attending to all sounds, inside and outside, as material for music and healing. Her framing of listening as a political, creative, and bodily act is directly relevant to SENAIDA's practice.

## Gesture, body, and music interfaces

A newly emerging research direction, anchored by the Low Res MFA thesis: how does gesture — the moving body — interface with and produce sound? The [[sources/notion-reading-list]] contains 15+ NIME papers on gesture mapping, motion-controlled instruments, embodied cognition in musical performance, and deformable sensor interfaces. This is adjacent to biofeedback (physiological data) but more specifically about intentional movement as instrument. The "Body As Instrument" reading is the thesis statement of the whole cluster.

## Club culture theory

Beyond practice, a growing theoretical engagement with the sociology and politics of club culture. Michelle Lhooq's post-cringe psychedelic spirituality essay is representative: the spiritual core of rave (collective transcendence, ego dissolution, ritual time) being simultaneously commodified and academically legitimated; the Berghain critique; the difference between collective ritual and individual therapeutic psychedelic experience. This is theoretical grounding for SENAIDA's self-framing as "shaman of the dancefloor."
