# Identity

SENAIDA (pronounced *shuh-nay-duh*) is Hakka Chinese-Canadian, born July 16, 2002 in Toronto. They are queer, and use they/them pronouns. Older press coverage (pre-2024) sometimes used she/her; their current and preferred usage is they/them throughout. The name itself is used as both a given name (Senaida Ng) and a mononym (SENAIDA) — the uppercase mononym is the performance and public-facing identity.

**Astrological profile:** Sun in Cancer, Moon in Libra, Rising Scorpio. The Cancer-Libra-Scorpio combination is notable: Cancer as the emotional core and home-orientation (a fixed longing for rootedness despite constant movement); Libra Moon as the relational and aesthetic intelligence that mediates everything through beauty and balance; Scorpio Rising as the face the world meets — magnetic, intense, and controlled in what it reveals. Whether SENAIDA works with astrology as a system is developing as an interest.

Their identity is explicitly multiple and layered: transdisciplinary artist, sound architect, researcher, creative entrepreneur, cyberfeminist, futurist, archivist. These aren't just labels — each reflects a real sustained practice. The RA bio offers one crystallization: "shaman of the dancefloor," a phrase that sits at the intersection of ritual, community, and the particular power they locate in club culture.

## Heritage

Hakka identity is a recurring, deliberate reference in SENAIDA's work. The Hakka people are a Han Chinese ethnic group historically defined by migration — "Hakka" itself translates roughly to "guest people." This etymology is explicitly mobilized in the project *<Hakka> means Guest People*, a sonic guide that appears to explore the experience of cultural displacement and belonging through sound. The resonance with their own life as a diasporic artist — moving between Toronto, NYC, and Berlin, rooted everywhere and nowhere — is evident and presumably intentional.

## Geographic Self

SENAIDA identifies as working between New York and Berlin, rooted in the underground scenes of both cities. Toronto is origin; NYC (specifically Brooklyn) is home base and scene; Berlin is the place that changed everything — studying abroad there in 2022 was the pivot point where classical training met rave culture and the DJ practice began.

As of May 2026, the relationship to Berlin is more ambivalent. SENAIDA moved there to pursue DJing and the sound healing community, has a nice room and feels relatively free — but describes not loving the city, specifically citing an excess of white people in its spaces. The primary reason for being in Berlin is structural (career proximity to the European club circuit, Jason as roommate and co-founder) rather than genuine love of place. The stated ideal is Goa or Thailand or Greece — somewhere with warmth, community, and the capacity for a different kind of life.

## Classical training as root identity

SENAIDA began classical piano at age 4. The Royal Conservatory of Music ARCT designation signals a level of technical accomplishment that most people never reach. This is the deepest and longest-standing identity thread — older than the DJ practice, older than the research career, older than the cyberfeminist politics. The Life Audit flags the six-year absence from the piano as a significant rupture: the identity of "classical musician" is maintained without the practice that gives it substance. The relationship to piano is described as something to restore, not to abandon.

## Identities in tension

SENAIDA holds several identities that might seem in tension: a classically trained concert pianist who plays techno; an academic researcher who works in clubs; a queer BIPOC community builder who also tours internationally as a solo artist. These tensions appear to be generative rather than uncomfortable — their practice is defined by refusing to separate what others keep apart.

The Life Audit introduces a harder version of this tension: the gap between the performed artist identity and the actual creative practice. The identities that are publicly visible (touring DJ, published researcher, founder) are all real — and yet the deepest private practice (making music alone, for no one) has been evacuated. The question of which identity is more "real" is both a psychological and an identity question.

## Political dimension

Identity for SENAIDA is explicitly political. Cyberfeminism, queer BIPOC community-building, and the critique of exploitative systems in music and technology are not separate from artistic practice — they are the substance of it. Family of East (who selected them for Asian Tones' Ones to Watch 2026) described them as "so much more than an artist, exploring sound through multiple mediums of art and technology."

## Post-individual structure

SENAIDA's identity is not singular but constitutionally multiple — a textbook post-individual in Yancey Strickler's sense: someone who has accepted their individuality in its full multiplicity and is now building collective forms around its various facets. Club culture, research, startup, collective organizing, Hakka heritage research — each is a community of micro-persona, not a contradiction to resolve.

The *yosotros* (I-we) concept is especially apt: SENAIDA exists most fully not as a solo subject but in the moments where the individual and collective are indistinguishable — on the dancefloor, in a biofeedback session, in a collective organizing moment. See [[Concepts/Post-Individualism]].

## Hakka Futurism as identity project

The **Hakka Futurism** research project (active in Notion) represents SENAIDA's most sustained attempt to build a theoretical framework from their diasporic identity rather than borrowing one from elsewhere. The Hakka — nomadic "guest people" defined by migration and cultural resilience — offer a philosophical orientation to technology and the future that Western futurisms cannot. This is a live and important project. See [[Concepts/Hakka-Futurism]].
