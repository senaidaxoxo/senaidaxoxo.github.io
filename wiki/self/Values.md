# Values

SENAIDA's values are unusually visible — they're embedded directly in the work rather than just stated alongside it. The best evidence comes from what they repeatedly choose to build: queer BIPOC spaces, fair-compensation music systems, community-owned clubs, technology that empowers creators rather than extracting from them.

## Collective experience and community

Across every domain of their work — clubs, research, entrepreneurship, art installations — SENAIDA builds toward collective experience. WE ARE THE FUTURE! is not just a party; it's explicitly a space for community members to share what they're working on and connect. The Youth Showcase was for artists under 25. The GEM Club grew to 300+ members. MiSynth was designed to democratize music creation. KUNST KAPUTT splits revenue 50-50 with whoever remixes it. The value isn't individual success — it's what happens when people with shared stakes gather in a room (or on a dancefloor) together.

## Democratization of technology and creative access

A recurring concern is who gets to make music and art, and on whose terms. The MiSynth vision was explicitly about making music production accessible to people who can't translate their inner sound into existing instruments. The cyberfeminism framework locates the problem structurally: data teams composed of dominant-group members produce tools that serve dominant-group users. The intervention is building different tools for different communities. KUNST KAPUTT's blockchain model is another expression of the same value: removing the intermediary that extracts from artists.

## AI as amplification, not replacement

This is a deliberate position, not just a rhetorical hedge. KUNST KAPUTT demonstrates it — the album uses AI as a creative collaborator and a mechanism for extending the work beyond SENAIDA's own participation. The Frontiers paper also frames the AGM-AI system as enhancing human connection, not simulating it. The core belief, made explicit in the Mixmag Asia profile: "AI's potential as a tool to amplify, rather than replace, human creativity."

## Experimentation and refusal of category

The bios consistently describe "genre-defying" and "interdisciplinary" — this is a value, not just a description. SENAIDA's range (classical → techno → visual art → research → entrepreneurship) reflects a deliberate refusal to let any single institution, genre, or audience define them. The cyberfeminist theory connects here too: Glitch Feminism's "glitch as refusal" is about declining to perform within broken systems, not optimizing within them.

## Ritual and the sacred

A surprising but consistent thread. The phrase "shaman of the dancefloor" is not ironic — SENAIDA describes clubbing as a "sacred ritual" and DJing as "expressing a moment's essence." The art practice draws on shamanism and posthumanism as frameworks. Sustainability and the spiritual are linked in their work on *The Last Rice Plant Harvested By Human Hands*. This isn't mysticism for its own sake; it's a serious claim that collective sonic experience has ritual power.

## Stated vs. enacted

The values stated above appear consistently enacted in the work. If there are gaps, they're not visible in public sources — this wiki will need journal entries and direct reflection to surface any tensions between what SENAIDA says matters and what their choices actually prioritize.
